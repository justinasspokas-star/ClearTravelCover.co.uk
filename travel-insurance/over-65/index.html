Older traveller insurance guide

Travel insurance for over 65s

Age does not remove the need to compare carefully. Focus on medical screening, trip limits, activities and the real value of your booking.

Reviewed 14 September 2026Published by Justinas SpokasEditorial policy
Quick answer

Travel insurance remains available to many people over 65, but providers can apply different age limits, medical questions, prices and maximum trip durations. Specialist insurers or brokers may help when standard products are unsuitable.

Why age changes the comparison

Some annual policies have upper age limits while single-trip products may accept older travellers. The right comparison is therefore not simply the cheapest quote: check whether the policy accepts every traveller’s age, health, destination and journey length.

MoneyHelper recommends researching beyond comparison sites because specialist providers may not appear everywhere. If finding cover is difficult, its medical travel insurance directory or an insurance broker can provide another route.

Medical screening

Declare all conditions requested by the insurer accurately, along with medication, recent treatment and ongoing investigations. Answer for every traveller on the policy. If health changes between purchase and departure, contact the insurer and follow the policy instructions.

Read the final medical endorsement or schedule carefully. It should make clear which declared conditions are covered, excluded or subject to special terms.

What older travellers should compare

  • Emergency medical expenses: understand limits, assistance and repatriation terms.
  • Trip duration: confirm that every day is covered, especially for long winter stays.
  • Cancellation: match the limit to the value of each person’s non-refundable bookings.
  • Mobility equipment: check limits for wheelchairs, walking aids or medical devices.
  • Travel companions: understand how one person’s health could affect another traveller’s claim.
  • Activities and cruises: add appropriate cover where required.

Single-trip or annual cover?

Annual policies can be convenient for frequent travellers, but age and maximum-trip limits often deserve close attention. A single-trip policy may provide a more precise fit for a longer holiday or a particular medical situation. Compare both options using identical traveller details.

Travelling with a companion

If a relative or friend is travelling with you, check how the policy handles cancellation or curtailment caused by the health of an insured traveller, travelling companion or close relative. Definitions and evidence requirements vary.

If standard insurers decline cover

A declined quote does not necessarily mean every provider will decline. Specialist medical insurers assess risks differently. MoneyHelper maintains a directory of firms authorised and regulated by the FCA that focus on serious medical conditions.

Frequently asked questions

Is there one universal age limit?

No. Each policy sets its own eligibility and may distinguish between single-trip and annual insurance.

Must every condition be declared?

Answer every medical screening question fully and accurately. If uncertain, contact the insurer before purchasing.

Can over-80s obtain cover?

Some specialist providers offer policies for older ages, subject to their assessment and terms.
