UK to Spain travel guide

Travel insurance for Spain

A practical guide to choosing cover for a Spanish holiday, city break, cruise or longer stay — and understanding where a GHIC fits in.

Reviewed 14 September 2026Published by Justinas SpokasEditorial policy
Quick answer

A UK GHIC can help with medically necessary state healthcare in Spain, but it is not a substitute for travel insurance. A suitable policy may also cover cancellation, baggage, private treatment where eligible and medical repatriation, subject to its terms.

Do UK travellers need travel insurance for Spain?

Travel insurance is not simply about meeting an entry requirement. It is designed to protect against eligible costs that can arise before or during a trip. The UK government recommends arranging appropriate insurance as soon as you book and checking that it covers every destination, the full trip duration and planned activities.

For Spain, compare the policy against the real value of your bookings and the way you intend to travel. A quiet weekend in Madrid presents different considerations from a family resort holiday, a cruise calling at Spanish ports or a hiking trip in the Canary Islands.

GHIC and healthcare in Spain

A valid UK GHIC or eligible EHIC can provide access to medically necessary state healthcare during a temporary stay. Treatment is provided on the same basis as for people insured in Spain, which means some services may still involve charges.

GHIC does not generally cover private healthcare, mountain rescue or the cost of returning you to the UK. Those gaps are why government guidance recommends carrying both the card and suitable travel insurance.

Remember: keep your GHIC details accessible, but contact your insurer's emergency assistance service promptly if you need treatment.

What should a Spain policy include?

  • Emergency medical expenses and repatriation: review the limit, assistance process and exclusions.
  • Cancellation and curtailment: make sure the cover reflects non-refundable flights, accommodation and activities.
  • Baggage and valuables: compare the total limit and the maximum payable for one item.
  • Personal liability: check what is covered if you accidentally injure someone or damage property.
  • Activities: declare or add cover for hiking, cycling, water sports, golf, cruises or winter sports when relevant.
  • Excess: understand how much you contribute to each type of claim.

Single-trip or annual cover?

Single-trip insurance can suit one defined Spanish holiday. Annual multi-trip insurance may be worth comparing if you expect several trips during the year, but check the geographical area, maximum length of each trip and age restrictions. Do not assume every “Europe” policy treats cruises, islands or longer stays in the same way.

Medical conditions and medicines

Answer medical screening questions accurately and declare existing physical or mental health conditions, including conditions under investigation when the insurer asks about them. If your health changes after purchasing, follow the policy instructions and contact the provider. Carry medicines in appropriate packaging and check official rules for taking controlled medicines abroad.

Spain travel insurance checklist

  1. Check current FCDO advice for the part of Spain you will visit.
  2. Apply for or renew your GHIC through the official NHS service.
  3. Calculate the total non-refundable value of the trip.
  4. List every planned activity and cruise segment.
  5. Compare exclusions and excesses, not only the price.
  6. Save the emergency assistance number and policy documents.

Frequently asked questions

Does GHIC make insurance unnecessary?

No. GHIC focuses on eligible state healthcare and does not replace wider travel insurance protections.

When should I buy cover?

Buying soon after booking may allow eligible cancellation protection to begin earlier, subject to the policy terms.

Will one policy cover mainland Spain and its islands?

Often, but confirm the geographical definition and trip details with the provider, especially for cruises or multi-destination itineraries.
