European travel insurance guide

Travel insurance for Europe

Plan cover around your complete European itinerary — including every country, activity, journey length and any existing medical conditions.

Reviewed 14 September 2026General information for UK travellers
Quick answer

European travel insurance can cover one holiday, several countries or multiple trips during a year. A GHIC is useful in participating countries but does not replace travel insurance for cancellation, baggage, private treatment or repatriation.

What does European travel insurance cover?

Coverage varies by provider and policy level. Common sections include emergency medical expenses, repatriation, cancellation, curtailment, baggage, personal liability and travel disruption. Limits, exclusions and excesses determine how useful those headings are in practice.

Check the geographical definition rather than relying on the word “Europe”. Insurers may group countries differently, and territories, cruises or countries outside the EU can have specific rules.

GHIC and EHIC in Europe

A valid UK GHIC or eligible EHIC can help you access medically necessary state healthcare in participating countries on local terms. It does not generally cover private care, repatriation, lost baggage or cancellation. For that reason, UK guidance recommends travelling with appropriate insurance as well as the card.

Single-trip, multi-country or annual?

  • Single trip: designed around one departure and return period.
  • Multi-country trip: must include every destination and stopover in the covered region.
  • Annual multi-trip: can cover repeated trips, subject to a maximum length for each journey.
  • Backpacking or long stay: may need a specialist product when standard duration limits are too short.

Activities that may need extra cover

Standard policies do not necessarily include winter sports, cruises, competitive sport, higher-altitude hiking, motorcycling or certain water activities. Read the activity list and add the correct extension where available. Protective equipment and supervision requirements may apply.

What to compare beyond price

Start with the non-refundable value of your bookings. Then compare medical and repatriation protection, cancellation limits, the single-item baggage limit, disruption benefits, excesses and exclusions. Check whether the policy covers airline or travel-provider failure if that matters to your booking.

European travel checklist

  1. List every country and transit stop.
  2. Check FCDO advice for each destination.
  3. Apply for or renew your GHIC through the official NHS route.
  4. Choose the correct trip duration and policy region.
  5. Declare medical conditions and planned activities.
  6. Keep policy and emergency contact details accessible.

Frequently asked questions

Is every European country covered by GHIC?

No. Eligibility and arrangements vary, so check official healthcare guidance for each destination.

Can one policy cover several European countries?

Yes, when the selected geographical area and itinerary include them and all policy conditions are met.

Is annual insurance always cheaper?

Not always. Compare the total price and suitability against separate single-trip policies, including trip-length and activity limits.

Official sources: UK foreign travel insurance guidance, FCDO country advice and the official NHS GHIC service.
